Rules of Manifestation

Learn the mechanics, master the battlefield, and discover how to harness the power of Essence.

Essetara: Manifest is currently in development. These rules describe the current prototype and may change as the game undergoes testing and refinement. Rules still being developed are marked In development.

      How to Use This Guide

      Start with the overview, components, Domains, battlefield and Essence. Follow the Action/Reaction timing sequence in Chapters 7 and 9. A specific ability can override a general rule when it explicitly says so.

      1. Introduction and Game Overview

      1.1 The World of Essetara

      Essetara is a dark-fantasy world of contested sites and supernatural Essence. Rival characters draw on reality’s underlying Essences to manifest abilities and pursue objectives.

      1.2 Objective of the Game

      Essetara: Manifest is an objective-driven tactical skirmish game played on a hex grid. Defeating opponents may help, but mission objectives determine victory.

      In development: Scenario-specific scoring, public objectives, possible hidden agendas and tie resolution are not yet fixed.

      1.3 Players and Characters

      The standard prototype pits two players against each other; each controls three characters. Characters have independent Domain values and markers on their owner’s Tempo track.

      1.4 Core Gameplay Concepts

      Players alternate activations, choosing any of their own non-Exhausted characters. Abilities may require current Domain thresholds and pay Essence and Catalyst costs. Terrain, the shared Environment, timing interrupts and individual Tempo tracks shape tactical choices.

      1.5 How to Use This Rulebook

      This guide describes the current rules. Content still in development is listed in Appendix B.

      2. Game Components and Board Layout

      2.1 Component Inventory

      Prototype components include:

      • a hex Battlefield
      • terrain features
      • three characters per player
      • character/ability cards
      • player decks
      • a shared Essence environment
      • character reserves
      • Tempo tracks
      • markers for Domains and conditions

      The shared Environment consists of 15 paired, double-sided Essence cards, all present throughout play. Each player has a 36-card Catalyst Deck. There is no separate Energy Deck.

      2.2 Battlefield and Terrain

      The Battlefield is divided into hexes. A map represents a specific location, such as a Forest Clearing, containing themed terrain features.

      2.3 Environment and Essence Cards

      The shared Environment contains 15 double-sided Essence cards, one for each unique pair of the six Essence types. All 15 are present and available subject to their current face and card-specific restrictions.

      When a face-up Environment Essence is used to pay an ability cost, flip that card after payment. The reverse face becomes the available Essence. There is no separate Energy Deck or replacement queue.

      2.4 Character and Ability Cards

      Character cards identify a character and track current and maximum Mind, Body and Spirit. Ability records include name, classification, timing, target, range, requirements, cost and effects.

      2.5 Catalyst Deck and Catalyst Cards

      Each player has a common 36-card Catalyst Deck, with six cards of each of the six Catalyst types and three copies of each of 12 Item/Tactical effects. There are no character-specific cards. Each card is used for its Catalyst contribution or its Item/Tactical effect, not both.

      2.6 Player Hand, Play Area, and Discard Pile

      Each player draws seven cards at game start. Subsequent draws occur individually only when an ability or effect instructs a draw. The Play Area displays committed cards and markers. Used cards are discarded after the Action or Reaction resolves.

      When the deck is empty and a card must be drawn, shuffle the discard pile to form the new deck. If both deck and discard pile are empty, skip the draw.

      2.7 Tempo Tracks and Markers

      Each player maintains a separate Tempo track with one marker per character.

      2.8 Character Reserves and Status Markers

      Each character has a hidden personal Reserve with two spaces for Essence drawn from terrain. Status markers track effects and Domain changes.

      Reserve is filled by abilities, passives or Item/Tactical effects. Up to both stored Essences may be spent on an ability in place of drawing matching Essence (see 5.7).

      3. Characters and Domains

      3.1 Character Cards

      A character’s card records identity, faction or affiliation, abilities and its three Domain pools. Life is not a separate health statistic.

      3.2 Mind, Body, and Spirit

      Mind, Body and Spirit are the three Domains. They represent distinct forms of resilience and serve as eligibility gates for abilities.

      3.3 Current and Maximum Domain Values

      Each Domain has a current value and maximum. Damage decreases the relevant current Domain; recovery restores it without exceeding the maximum.

      3.4 Domain Requirements

      Domain values are not spent as an ability cost. An ability may require Body ≥10, Body ≤3, or multiple thresholds such as Mind ≥8 AND Spirit ≥6. Eligibility is checked against current values.

      Check Domain Requirements when the ability is declared. A subsequent change in Domain values does not invalidate the ability on the basis of its Domain Requirements.

      Example: Domain Requirement

      A character with current Body 9 cannot use a Body ≥10 ability. Taking Body damage can enable an ability requiring Body ≤3; neither threshold spends Body points.

      3.5 Domain Damage and Recovery

      Damage and recovery are applied to the named Domain separately. A hit can deal, for example, 3 Body and 1 Spirit damage.

      Damage reduction specifies the Domains it can affect. If more than one Domain is eligible, the defender chooses one; reduction cannot be split unless explicitly permitted. Apply reduction before reducing the Domain value. Domain values cannot fall below zero and excess damage does not spill over. Check zero-Domain thresholds after the entire ability resolves (see 9.7).

      3.6 Passive Abilities

      Character-specific passive abilities may modify targeting, Domain thresholds, terrain interaction or timing, as described on the card.

      Triggered passives resolve at their printed timing. At the same timing, the Active player resolves first; each player chooses the order of their own simultaneous effects. Newly triggered interrupts resolve at their specified timing before the interrupted sequence continues.

      3.7 Incapacitation

      A character is Incapacitated when two of its three Domains reach 0. Its marker remains on its current Battlefield hex. It does not block Line of Sight, and other characters may move through or occupy that hex. A single Domain at 0 instead places the character in an Exhausted state on the Tempo track.

      Normal recovery ends Incapacitation when at least two Domains are above zero and no other character occupies its hex. Domain values may recover while the hex is occupied, but Incapacitation persists until the other character leaves. Three Domains at zero have no additional Incapacitation effect.

      An Incapacitated character cannot react. Once recovered, normal Exhaustion rules apply.

      4. Battlefield, Movement, and Line of Sight

      4.1 Hex Grid and Positioning

      Each character occupies one hex. Hexes are adjacent only when they share an edge. Hex distance counts the shortest number of adjacent-hex steps, excluding the origin.

      Allied characters may move through hexes occupied by allies but cannot end their movement in another active character’s hex. Characters may not move through hexes occupied by hostile characters. Incapacitated characters are an exception: their hex may be crossed and occupied.

      4.2 Movement and Movement Effects

      Movement proceeds one adjacent hex at a time, spending the entry cost of each hex. Forced movement uses the same rules as voluntary movement; only control of the path differs.

      Forced movement does not bypass hostile occupied hexes or impassable terrain unless a specific ability explicitly overrides those restrictions. Allied occupied hexes can be crossed under the normal occupancy rule.

      Forced movement triggers the same terrain, passive and movement effects as voluntary movement. An effect that ignores terrain ignores all terrain traits, including Severe entry costs, impassability and terrain triggers.

      4.3 Terrain Occupancy and Restrictions

      Terrain can be traversable, impassable or blocking; a terrain feature blocking Line of Sight does not automatically prevent occupation.

      Entering a normal hex costs one movement point; entering Severe Terrain costs two. A character must have sufficient movement remaining to enter the hex. These costs apply equally to forced and voluntary movement.

      If movement is interrupted in a hex occupied by an ally and cannot continue, the player controlling the movement chooses an adjacent legal hex in which to place the moving character. Movement cannot voluntarily end in an allied occupied hex.

      In development: Placement if no adjacent legal hex exists after interrupted movement.

      4.4 Line of Sight

      Draw a straight line from the center of the acting character’s hex to the center of the target hex. Line of Sight (LoS) is blocked if the line crosses the interior of a blocking terrain hex. Touching only a blocking hex edge or corner does not block LoS. A line following the shared edge of two adjacent blocking hexes is blocked.

      Characters do not ordinarily block Line of Sight. An Incapacitated character expressly does not block Line of Sight. For Area Effects, Line of Sight is checked to the chosen central hex, not to every affected hex.

      Example: Line of Sight

      If the center-to-center line touches only the corner of a blocking hex, LoS is clear. If it follows the shared edge of two blocking hexes, LoS is blocked.

      4.5 Range and Targeting

      Range is the shortest hex distance between source and target; the source hex is Range 0. An ability may target Self, Ally, Enemy, Character, Hex, Terrain or Area, subject to its printed restrictions.

      If a Reaction or interrupt invalidates a target, a legal replacement target may be selected. If no replacement is selected, target-dependent effects fail and independent effects continue. Paid costs are not refunded (see 7.9).

      4.6 Area Effects

      An Area Effect has a designated central hex and a specified shape. The player may rotate that shape around its central hex as allowed by the ability. Only the central hex requires Line of Sight; the other affected hexes do not need individual Line of Sight checks.

      The Area Effect template is printed on the ability. Most Area Effects target a hex rather than a character. A template may be rotated so that part extends beyond the Battlefield; only hexes on the Battlefield are affected.

      Rotation is limited to the six hex orientations. An Area Effect centered on a character remains at its original location if that character moves.

      4.7 Movement Interrupts

      Resolve movement one hex at a time.

      • Before effects resolve before the named event.
      • When effects interrupt at their trigger.
      • After effects follow completion of the named event.

      Movement is complete when no movement remains, the character cannot move farther, or the controlling player voluntarily stops in a legal hex. If interrupted in an allied occupied hex, apply the placement rule in 4.3. Completion effects then resolve according to their wording.

      Interrupts resolve at their specified timing. At the same timing, the Active player goes first; each player chooses the order of their own simultaneous interrupts. A newly triggered interrupt resolves before the interrupted sequence resumes (see 7.9).

      Example: Movement

      A five-point move is interrupted after two normal hexes by a When effect changing who controls movement. The remaining three points follow normal terrain and occupancy rules. Movement completes when no points remain, no further movement is possible, or the controller stops in a legal hex.

      5. Essence and Terrain

      5.1 Six Essence Types

      The six Essence types
      Essence Represents
      ElementalNatural elements and physical forces
      CyclicalLife, death, growth and decay
      CelestialTime, space, gravity and cosmic phenomena
      EquilibriumBalance, order, chaos and instability
      PsychicThought, emotion, memory and perception
      AethericSpirits, souls, bonds and supernatural planes

      5.2 Essence Sources

      Essence may be drawn from a shared Environment, eligible nearby terrain and a character’s private Reserve.

      5.3 Environment and Double-Sided Essence Cards

      The shared Environment always displays all 15 unique paired Essence cards. One face is available on each card. After a player uses that Essence to pay a cost, that card flips to its other Essence face.

      All 15 cards remain in the Environment; there is no separate Energy Deck, Environment capacity beyond 15, FIFO replacement or routine refill.

      5.4 Flipping and Altering Essence Cards

      Using an Environment card to pay a cost flips it after payment. An ability may alter, lock or otherwise interact with an Environment card only if its text explicitly permits that effect.

      The locking ability specifies lock duration. A locked card can be flipped by another effect only if that effect explicitly permits flipping locked cards. An ability requiring Essence that cannot be supplied cannot be performed.

      5.5 Terrain Essence and Map-Specific Terrain

      Each map represents one distinctive location. Map terrain features, such as a Forest Clearing’s Flower Patch, Fungal Colony, Hollow Tree and Vine Cluster, each provide access to three assigned Essence types. Map terrain has a shared depletion allowance of 10 total Essence draws, regardless of which of its three types is drawn.

      A character may draw one Essence from each eligible terrain feature per Action or Reaction. To qualify, the character must be within one hex of the terrain. Each draw consumes one of that feature’s remaining uses; the feature depletes after its tenth draw.

      In development: Map-specific terrain properties.

      5.6 Created Terrain

      Created terrain provides access to exactly two Essence types and has five total draws shared across those types. Each eligible character may draw one Essence from each eligible created terrain feature per Action or Reaction while within one hex.

      Created terrain cannot be placed on existing terrain. It cannot be placed beneath a character unless the creating ability specifically targets a character.

      Created terrain depletes after five total Essence draws; map terrain after ten. Depleted terrain remains physical terrain and retains its applicable terrain properties.

      Players may track remaining draws using counters, dice or written records. Removed terrain supplies no Essence. Replacement terrain begins with its full draw allowance.

      In development: Created-terrain duration.

      5.7 Character Reserves

      Each character has a private Reserve with two spaces for storing Essence drawn from terrain.

      Abilities, passives and Item/Tactical effects fill the Reserve. Either or both stored Essences may be spent on an ability in place of drawing Essence of the same type.

      Filling a Reserve consumes terrain draws and follows normal range and per-feature limits unless explicitly overridden. Stored Essence cannot be swapped or replaced until used. Effects and abilities may reveal stored Essence.

      5.8 Drawing and Using Essence

      An Action or Reaction may draw one Essence from each eligible terrain feature within one hex of its character.

      • Map terrain provides one of three Essence types per draw (10 total draws per feature).
      • Created terrain provides one of two (5 total draws per feature).
      • Environment Essence is taken from the face-up side of one of 15 cards and flipped after it pays a cost.
      • Reserve: up to two matching stored Essences may supplement the cost from the character’s Reserve.

      Environment use is optional and has no numerical draw limit; all, some or none of the required Essence may come from the Environment. Do not draw Essence that will not be used.

      An ability cannot be performed if its required Essence cannot be supplied from legal sources.

      5.9 Essence Substitution

      Effects, Passive Abilities and Item/Tactical effects may explicitly permit substitution of one Essence type for another. Catalyst symbols are separate from Essence.

      6. Catalyst Cards

      6.1 Catalyst Card Overview

      Catalyst Cards belong to each player’s Catalyst Deck. Each card may be used either for its Catalyst contribution or for its printed Item/Tactical option, never both in the same use.

      A Manifestation is an ability whose costs have been paid; Manifest and Manifestation do not name a card or deck.

      6.2 Six Catalysts

      The six Catalysts
      Catalyst Represents
      ForcePower or impact
      MotionPosition or movement
      TimeDuration or temporal change
      MagnitudeIntensity
      ShapeForm or area
      FlexAlteration or adaptation

      In development: Catalyst mechanical definitions and icons (see 12.2).

      6.3 Using Cards for Catalysts

      Abilities pay their listed Catalyst requirements using eligible Catalyst Cards. The Catalyst symbols and Essence draws are distinct resources. A card used for its Catalyst cannot also activate its Item/Tactical effect.

      A Passive Ability, effect or Item/Tactical effect may explicitly allow a Catalyst Card to supply multiple symbols or be allocated across manifestations.

      6.4 Item and Tactical Effects

      Item/Tactical effects are printed on Catalyst Cards and have their own costs. The initial deck contains 12 distinct Item/Tactical effects, with three copies of each.

      In development: The 12 Item/Tactical effects and their distribution.

      6.5 Dual-Use Restriction

      A Catalyst Card may be used for its Catalyst contribution or its printed Item/Tactical effect, never both for the same use.

      6.6 Hand and Discard Procedures

      At game start each player draws seven cards. Subsequent draws occur individually as specified by an effect. Used cards are discarded after the Action or Reaction resolves.

      When the deck is empty and a card must be drawn, shuffle the discard pile to form the new deck.

      If both the deck and discard pile are empty, skip the draw.

      7. Abilities and Manifestation

      7.1 Ability Card Anatomy

      An ability lists Name, Type, Tempo, Target, Range, Domain Requirement, Essence Cost, Catalyst Cost, timing and effects. There are currently no ability tiers. Damage specifies both Domain and amount.

      7.2 Action and Reaction Abilities

      Actions are declared by the acting character. Reactions are declared in response to an Action and resolve according to their printed effects and requirements.

      Reaction Tempo is specified by the ability. An Exhausted character may only use Defensive Reactions against Actions targeting it. Incapacitated characters cannot react.

      7.3 Attack, Defense, and Tactical Classifications

      Ability classifications under consideration are Offensive, Defensive, Motion and Tactical. Each ability specifies its classification; an Item/Tactical option may have its own timing.

      In development: Final ability classifications.

      7.4 Requirements and Costs

      Domain values determine eligibility and are never spent. Essence costs are paid with individual Essence draws and Catalyst costs with eligible Catalyst Cards. Tempo is a separate printed cost.

      An ability requiring unavailable Essence cannot be performed. Players cannot draw extra Essence that will not be used.

      7.5 Declaring and Targeting

      1. The Active player selects a non-Exhausted character, declares an ability, selects a target and pays the Essence and Catalyst costs.
      2. Active Immediately effects then resolve.
      3. The defender may declare a legal Reaction, choose target and pay costs.
      4. Reaction Immediately effects resolve.
      5. Remaining effects resolve by printed timing, Active first in a shared ordinary window.

      Explicit interrupts trigger before other effects continue.

      Interrupts resolve at their printed timing. If a Reaction or interrupt invalidates a target, a legal replacement may be selected; otherwise target-dependent effects fail and independent effects continue. Paid costs are not refunded (see 7.9).

      7.6 Drawing Essence and Committing Catalyst Cards

      The acting player draws eligible Essence and commits Catalyst Cards to pay the declared ability cost. Each eligible terrain feature within one hex can supply one Essence per Action/Reaction. Environment cards flip after their face-up Essence is used to pay a cost.

      Terrain draw limit: one Essence per eligible feature per Action or Reaction; map terrain has ten total draws and created terrain five. Up to two matching Reserve Essences may be spent. Environment draws are optional and have no numerical limit. Draw only Essence needed for the cost.

      Used Catalyst Cards are discarded after the Action or Reaction resolves or ends. Paid costs are not refunded when a target becomes invalid.

      7.7 Manifestation and Effect Resolution

      Resolve Immediately effects in the prescribed early window, followed by remaining effects in their specified timing windows. In the same ordinary timing window, Active-player effects resolve before Defending-player effects. Explicit interrupts resolve when their trigger conditions are met, before other effects continue.

      At the same timing, the Active player resolves first. Each player chooses the order of their own simultaneous effects. Newly triggered interrupts resolve at their specified timing before the next pending effect.

      7.8 Timing Keywords: Immediately, Before, When, After

      Immediately effects from the active ability resolve after the active player pays costs and before the defending player declares a Reaction. The defending Reaction’s Immediately effects resolve after its costs are paid. Other effects follow Before, When and After wording, with Active-player effects first in a shared ordinary window.

      An interrupt resolves before the interrupted effect continues. An Immediately effect can normally be interrupted only before it resolves. An ability may explicitly permit retroactive cancellation.

      7.9 Timing Priority and Interrupts

      Timing order:

      1. Active declaration, target and payment
      2. Active Immediately
      3. Defending declaration, target and payment
      4. Defending Immediately
      5. Remaining effects by printed timing

      At the same timing, the Active player goes first; each player orders their own simultaneous effects. Newly triggered interrupts resolve before continuation.

      If a Reaction or interrupt invalidates a target, a legal replacement may be selected. If no replacement is selected, only target-dependent effects fail; independent effects continue. Costs are not refunded.

      In development: The timing and controller of replacement target selection.

      8. Tempo and Activation

      8.1 Tempo Track Setup

      Each player has an independent Tempo track. During setup each player places three character markers in distinct positions among the first five spaces.

      8.2 Activation Order

      Players alternate activations. On their activation, a player may select any of their non-Exhausted characters; there is no requirement to activate the rearmost marker. Incapacitated characters cannot activate. If all characters controlled by a player are Exhausted or Incapacitated, the game ends.

      A character enters Exhaustion when a Domain reaches zero or when its Tempo marker is five or more spaces ahead of its nearest ally. See 8.5 for recovery.

      8.3 Tempo Costs

      Each Action or Reaction specifies its Tempo cost. Advance the character’s marker by the specified cost after resolution.

      An Action or Reaction that ends early counts as completed; apply its Tempo cost at completion as normal.

      8.4 Advancing Character Markers

      Each character advances on its owner’s track independently. Tempo positions determine readiness and exhaustion relative to allied markers.

      8.5 Exhaustion

      A character becomes Exhausted when five or more spaces ahead of its nearest ally or when a Domain reaches zero. Exhaustion clears when another allied character is less than five spaces behind on the Tempo Track. Exhausted characters cannot take Actions.

      An Exhausted character may take only Defensive Reactions against Actions targeting it. Once Exhausted, normal Exhaustion rules apply, including when a Domain remains at zero.

      Example: Tempo

      A marker five spaces ahead of its closest ally becomes Exhausted. When an ally is less than five spaces behind, normal Exhaustion clearance applies.

      In development: The precise Tempo marker adjustment, if any, when a Domain reaches zero.

      8.6 Continuous Activations

      Play proceeds through continuous alternating activations, without formal rounds. An activation ends when all effects of its Action and Reaction have resolved; the next activation then begins.

      9. Combat and Reactions

      9.1 Attack Resolution

      The Active player selects a non-Exhausted character, declares the attack, selects an eligible target and pays costs. Active Immediately effects then resolve. The Defender may declare and pay for a qualifying Reaction, after which its Immediately effects resolve. Other effects follow timing wording, Active player first in a shared ordinary window.

      All attacks permit a Reaction unless explicitly stated otherwise. If a Reaction invalidates a target, apply the replacement and continuation rules in 7.9. Paid costs are not refunded.

      9.2 Defensive Reactions

      An Exhausted character may only make a Defensive Reaction when it is the target of the Action. Incapacitated characters cannot react. A Reaction is declared and paid after the active ability’s Immediately effects, unless a printed interrupt explicitly overrides the sequence.

      There are no default Defend or Dodge effects. Characters use only the Passive Abilities and abilities listed on their character cards. Reaction Tempo is specified by the ability.

      9.3 Offensive and Tactical Reactions

      Reactions may modify a pending action where a specific ability allows them.

      Offensive and Tactical Reactions are legal in response to an Action, subject to their requirements and the restrictions on Exhausted and Incapacitated characters.

      9.4 Damage by Domain

      Apply each damage entry to its named Domain; e.g., 3 Body and 1 Spirit are two distinct damage entries.

      Check zero-Domain thresholds after resolution of the entire ability. One Domain reaching zero causes Exhaustion; two or more cause Incapacitation. Excess damage does not spill over. Recovery follows 3.7 and 8.5.

      9.5 Damage Redirection

      An ability may redirect damage between Domains or characters only when explicitly stated.

      Redirected damage is not a new damage event, but may trigger effects that respond to damage to the newly affected Domain.

      9.6 Interrupts and Timing Priority

      An interrupt resolves at its stated timing before the interrupted sequence continues. At the same timing, the Active player resolves first; each player orders their own simultaneous interrupts.

      If an interrupt triggers another interrupt, resolve the new interrupt at its specified timing before continuing with pending effects.

      9.7 Simultaneous Effects and Edge Cases

      At the same timing, the Active player resolves first; each player orders their own simultaneous effects. For an effect damaging multiple Domains or targets, check Incapacitation after the entire effect rather than after each damage entry. Zero-Domain checks occur at the end of the ability.

      An Incapacitated character remains in its hex but does not obstruct movement or LoS. It may contest or score objectives if a scenario or ability permits it.

      10. Conditions and Status Effects

      10.1 Conditions Overview

      Conditions are ongoing beneficial or harmful effects attached to characters or hexes and tracked with markers.

      10.2 Beneficial and Harmful Conditions

      Conditions may be beneficial or harmful. Opposing Conditions can coexist.

      10.3 Applying and Removing Conditions

      An ability or terrain effect states which condition it applies or removes and when it resolves.

      Opposing Conditions coexist rather than canceling each other.

      10.4 Duration and Expiration

      Duration is specified by the condition or source effect.

      A turn or activation ends when all effects of the Action and Reaction have resolved. The next turn or activation then begins.

      10.5 Stacking and Reapplication

      A Condition stacks only if its rules explicitly permit stacking. Reapplication does not refresh or replace an existing Condition.

      10.6 Terrain and Aura Effects

      A terrain feature or aura may apply effects when a character enters, exits, occupies or remains in an affected hex.

      Forced movement triggers the same terrain and aura effects as voluntary movement unless an effect explicitly overrides them.

      When an effect states that a character gains a Condition, it remains applied until removed according to its rules. When an effect states that a character suffers from a Condition within an Area of Effect, the Condition ends when the character leaves that area.

      11. Victory Conditions

      11.1 Scenario Objectives

      Scenarios determine the objectives that players contest; examples considered include capture, escort/kidnap and recovery.

      Scenario rules specify setup, objective timing and scoring.

      In development: Scenario deployment, scoring, tie-breaks and hidden agendas.

      11.2 Winning and Losing

      Victory follows the scenario’s win conditions. The game also ends if all characters controlled by a player are Exhausted or Incapacitated.

      In development: The winner determination when the game ends because all characters of a player are Exhausted or Incapacitated.

      11.3 Incapacitation and Elimination

      An Incapacitated character remains on its hex, which can be crossed or occupied, and does not block LoS. Recovery follows 3.7. It may contest or score objectives if the scenario or an ability permits it.

      Scenario elimination scoring depends on incapacitation and victory criteria.
